Alpha-beta on a 4-ply tree

The win for today: trace α-β through a full 4-ply exam tree, nodes A to O, and get every cut right, including cuts that remove whole subtrees.

The layout

4 ply means four levels of moves, alternating ▲ ▼ ▲ ▼, with 2 children per node:

LevelNodesPlayerTakes
0A▲ MAXmax
1B, C▼ MINmin
2D, E, F, G▲ MAXmax
3H … O▼ MINmin
416 leavesvalues given

Remember from our last talk: depth-first, left to right, not row by row. α and β go down; only v comes up. ▲ prunes when v ≥ β; ▼ prunes when v ≤ α.[slide 30]

Watch it

Press Next. Before each click, say out loud what α and β will be at the next node. The highlighted node is where the algorithm is now, and faded nodes have been pruned.

Four kinds of cut happen in this tree. Look for each:
1. A leaf under I (▼): I sees 2 while α = 3 from H, so 2 ≤ 3 and its second leaf is skipped.
2. Node K, a whole subtree: E (▲) gets 15 from J, which is ≥ β = 3 inherited from B. MIN at B would never let play reach E’s other moves, so K is never visited.
3. A leaf under M: a cut deep in the right half, using α = 3 passed all the way down from A.
4. Node G, a whole subtree: C (▼) gets 3 from F, which is ≤ α = 3. Ties prune.
Root value: 3. Only 8 of 16 leaves examined.

Your turn

A new 4-ply tree, same layout. On paper, write α and β beside each node as you go. Then click the top of each pruned branch and press Check. If you click a leaf, that leaf alone is pruned; if you click a lettered node, its whole subtree is.

Hint, only if you’re stuck: there are 4 cuts, two of them whole lettered subtrees. The root value is not 3 this time.
