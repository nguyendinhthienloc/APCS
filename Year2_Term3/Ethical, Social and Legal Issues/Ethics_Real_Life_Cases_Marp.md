# Case 1: Doxxing & Reputation Attacks
![bg right:40% 80%](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1611162617474-5b21e879e113?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

- **Scenario:** A student is falsely accused of cheating. Their ID, phone, and name are posted online.
- **The Spread:** Goes viral overnight. Classmates and strangers share it. Victim receives hate.
- **Question:** When sharing unverified content harms someone, who is morally responsible?

---

# Case 1: Ethical Analysis

- ⚖️ **Kantianism (FAIL):** Treating victims as clickbait, not humans. Fails universal trust.
- 🔢 **Act Utilitarianism (WRONG):** Harm (trauma) vastly outweighs viral entertainment.
- 🌏 **Cultural Relativism (TENSION):** Shaming is culturally normalized, but real harm remains.
- 📜 **Social Contract (FAIL):** Breaks implicit rule: don't weaponize private data.
- 🌱 **Virtue Ethics (WRONG):** Lacks honesty and fairness.
- ✅ **Verdict:** Spreading unverified doxxing content is universally morally wrong.

# Case 2: AI Training Data Scraped
![bg right:40% 80%](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1620712943543-bcc4688e7485?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

- **Scenario:** AI company scrapes billions of artworks/writings from the web without consent.
- **The Conflict:** AI competes with creators. Company claims "fair use".
- **Question:** Is scraping public work without consent ethically justified just because it’s legal?

---

# Case 2: Ethical Analysis

- ⚖️ **Kantianism (FAIL):** Uses creators merely as data sources.
- 🔢 **Act Utilitarianism (SPLIT):** Massive user benefit vs. long-term harm to creative industry.
- 🌏 **Cultural Relativism (CONTESTED):** Western 'fair use' differs from cultures stressing authorship.
- 📜 **Social Contract (FAIL):** Breaks implicit trust of publishing work.
- 🌱 **Virtue Ethics (WRONG):** Lacks honesty (no disclosure) and fairness (no pay).
- ✅ **Verdict:** Technical legality ≠ moral justification. Ethical AI requires consent.

# Case 3: Attendance Favour
![bg right:40% 80%](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1612872087720-bb876e2e67d1?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

- **Scenario:** 20 out of 40 students skip PE class. The absent students ask the monitor to mark them present.
- **The Context:** The lecturer is lenient. The core goal is simply to get IT students exercising.
- **Question:** Is falsifying attendance acceptable if the students are otherwise resting or doing their own workouts?

---

# Case 3: Ethical Analysis

- 🔢 **Act Utilitarianism (ACCEPTABLE):** The core goal (health) is met. Resting or self-exercising maximizes well-being for exhausted IT students without causing direct harm.
- 🌏 **Cultural Relativism (TENSION):** 'Tình bạn' (friendship) justifies the favor, though lying to authority remains a mild taboo.
- ⚖️ **Kantianism (FAIL):** Intentions and physical health don't excuse the act of lying. 
- 📜 **Social Contract (FAIL):** Still a unilateral breach of the institutional "show up, get credit" rule.
- 🌱 **Virtue Ethics (WRONG):** A virtuous person does not use deception to achieve a healthy outcome.

---

# Theory Comparison

| Theory | Doxxing | AI Scraping | Attendance |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| ⚖️ Kantianism | ❌ Wrong | ❌ Wrong | ❌ Wrong |
| 📜 Social Contract | ❌ Wrong | ❌ Wrong | ❌ Wrong |
| 🌱 Virtue Ethics | ❌ Wrong | ❌ Wrong | ❌ Wrong |
| 🌏 Cultural Relativism | ⚠️ Tension | ⚠️ Contested | ⚠️ Tension |
| 🔢 Act Utilitarianism | ❌ Wrong | ⚠️ Split | ✅ Acceptable |

**Pattern:** Rule-based frameworks (Kant, Contract) remain strictly opposed. Utilitarianism flexes to "Acceptable" when the core intent (health) is met without harm.

---

# Three Key Takeaways

1. **Good intentions ≠ ethical actions:** Intent alone doesn't satisfy strict ethical frameworks like Kantianism.
2. **Context can shift the math:** Utilitarianism allows flexibility if the ultimate goal (e.g., student health/rest) is achieved without collateral harm.
3. **Multiple lenses sharpen judgment:** When frameworks disagree, it reveals exactly where our values are in conflict and sparks the best debates.
