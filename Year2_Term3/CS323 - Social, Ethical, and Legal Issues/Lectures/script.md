### Slide 3: Case 1: The Beatles Catalog Acquisition (1985)
**Speaker:** 
"In 1985, Michael Jackson bought ATV Music Publishing, which owned 251 Beatles songs, for 47.5 million dollars, outbidding Paul McCartney. Jackson later licensed tracks like 'Revolution' to Nike for commercial ads, upsetting McCartney. This raises the question: Is it ethical to buy, commodify, and license another artist's labor against their artistic intentions?"

---

### Slide 4: Case 1: Ethical & Legal Analysis
**Speaker:** 
"Looking at Case 1: Kantianism fails because Jackson treated McCartney's labor merely as a tool for financial profit. Act Utilitarianism is split: it generated massive wealth for Jackson but caused emotional distress to the original songwriters. Cultural Relativism is contested; it is standard capitalistic practice but violates artistic community norms. The Social Contract fails by breaking the implicit cooperative agreement of friendship. Finally, Virtue Ethics fails due to a lack of loyalty and respect. Legally, the transaction was valid, but ethically it is highly compromised."

---

### Slide 5: Case 2: The "Có Công Mài Sắc" AI Cover (2026)
**Speaker:** 
"In early 2026, Vietnamese singer Ngo Lan Huong released 'Có Công Mài Sắc'. Creators quickly remixed the song and used unauthorized AI vocal clones of other popular singers to perform covers, which went viral on TikTok. Ngo Lan Huong publicly urged fans to stop listening to these covers, noting they were emotionally flat and distorted her original performance. This raises the question of whether unauthorized AI voice cloning violates performer rights and artistic integrity."

---

### Slide 6: Case 2: Ethical & Legal Analysis
**Speaker:** 
"Analyzing Case 2: Kantianism fails because creators used the artists' voices and identities merely as tools for traffic without consent. Utilitarianism is split between brief listener entertainment and direct economic and creative harm to the original artists. Cultural Relativism is in tension between Gen Z's remix culture and traditional artists' rights. The Social Contract fails, violating Article 32 of the Vietnam Civil Code and Article 29 of the Vietnam IP Law. Virtue Ethics also fails for lacking honesty and respect for original creativity. Unauthorized AI covers are both legally and ethically unacceptable."

---

### Slide 7: Theory Comparison
**Speaker:** 
"This table summarizes how both cases evaluate under our theoretical frameworks. Both cases fail Kantianism, the Social Contract, and Virtue Ethics. Legally, there is a clear difference: Michael Jackson bought a contractual right to the music, while the AI creators infringed upon a fundamental personality right. However, morally they are the same, as both cases exploit the creators' identity and artistic labor without consent."

---

### Slide 8: Key Takeaways
**Speaker:** 
"To conclude: First, legality does not equal morality. Legal copyright ownership does not erase ethical obligations to original creators. Second, voice identity is protected under Vietnamese Law; unauthorized cloning violates personality and performance rights. Third, respecting creative integrity and the authentic performance is essential for a healthy creative ecosystem."
